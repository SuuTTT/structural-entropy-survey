## Related Work Mapping

### R1

**Origin**

Model paper section cue: “Community detection is a rich and challenging problem ...”

**Model**

- `TASK_DIFFICULTY`
- begin with the field family, not with a flat method list

**Rewrite**

SE belongs to a broader family of graph-complexity, graph-clustering, and graph-compression methods, but it differs from most classical objectives in treating hierarchical organization as the primary object to be decoded.

### R2

**Origin**

Model paper cue: “The most common approaches are based on ...”

**Model**

- `TAXONOMY_INTRO`
- introduce the dominant neighboring families before details

**Rewrite**

The closest reference points for understanding its role are modularity-based community detection, flow-based compression, spectral partitioning, and modern deep graph clustering. These neighboring lines of work are useful because they often pursue similar structural goals while relying on different assumptions about what counts as a good partition.

### R3

**Origin**

Model paper cue: “One assigns a score ... then looks for the division with the highest score.”

**Model**

- `FAMILY_1_MECHANISM`
- present a family through its optimization principle

**Rewrite**

Modularity-based community detection compares observed within-community edge density against a configuration-model null graph. Louvain and Leiden-style optimization make this objective highly scalable, and Leiden further improves partition connectivity and refinement stability.

### R4

**Origin**

Model paper cue: family mechanism followed by practical positioning

**Model**

- `ADVANTAGE_OR_TRADEOFF`

**Rewrite**

These methods remain strong default baselines for topology-driven clustering, but they are fundamentally flat and are known to exhibit resolution effects.

### R5

**Origin**

Model paper cue: define the specific objective after naming the family

**Model**

- `COMPARATIVE_NOTE`

**Rewrite**

SE differs in that it evaluates the coding cost induced by an encoding tree, so the partition is interpreted as an information hierarchy rather than only a density surplus.

### R6

**Origin**

Model paper cue: “A third major class ... links between community structure and dynamical processes ...”

**Model**

- `FAMILY_2_MECHANISM`

**Rewrite**

Flow-based compression methods such as the map equation and Infomap encode random-walk trajectories using module codebooks. They are conceptually close to SE because both explain graph organization through compression, but the object being compressed is different.

### R7

**Origin**

Model paper cue: “In the most popular method ... one considers the sequence ...”

**Model**

- `COMPARATIVE_NOTE`

**Rewrite**

Infomap compresses flows generated by random walks, whereas SE compresses structural uncertainty under graph volumes, cuts, and tree refinement.

### R8

**Origin**

Model paper cue: state why the family distinction matters conceptually

**Model**

- `ADVANTAGE_OR_TRADEOFF`

**Rewrite**

This difference becomes important when the graph is interpreted as an information system rather than an explicit navigation process.

### R9

**Origin**

Model paper style cue: once one family is covered, move to another by mechanism, not by chronology

**Model**

- `FAMILY_3_MECHANISM`

**Rewrite**

Spectral and cut-based methods use eigenvectors of graph Laplacians to expose low-conductance partitions and provide powerful relaxations for balanced graph partitioning.

### R10

**Origin**

Model paper family pattern: mechanism first, then what it does well, then what it misses

**Model**

- `ADVANTAGE_OR_TRADEOFF`

**Rewrite**

They remain important for initialization, diagnostics, and theoretical comparison. In most forms, however, spectral objectives are flat and externally parameterized by the target number of clusters.

### R11

**Origin**

**Model**

- `COMPARATIVE_NOTE`

**Rewrite**

SE can connect to spectral information, including the literature around von Neumann entropy, but its target remains a minimum-description hierarchy rather than an eigenspace partition alone.

**Model**

**Model**

- `UNIFYING_LINK`

**Rewrite**

Recent SE-based neural methods, including LSENet, SEP-style pooling, and DeSE, help bridge this gap by making structural criteria usable inside learned models.

### R15

**Origin**

Model paper cue from section endings: close by clarifying what the family map reveals about the field

**Model**

- `UNIFYING_LINK`
- synthesis close

**Rewrite**

One goal of the present survey is to place these neural methods on the same conceptual map as the original combinatorial SE algorithms and their domain-specific extensions. Taken together, these neighboring paradigms clarify what is distinctive about SE: it is not simply another flat clustering score, nor only a regularizer for graph neural networks, but a framework that understands graph organization through the coding efficiency of hierarchical structure.
